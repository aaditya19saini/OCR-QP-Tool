Q.9 Consider four coins labelled as 1,2,3 and 4. Suppose that the probability of obtaining a ‘head’ in a single toss of the \(i^{th}\) coin is \(\frac{i}{4}\), i = 1,2,3,4. A coin is chosen uniformly at random and flipped. Given that the flip resulted in a ‘head’, the conditional probability that the coin was labelled either 1 or 2 equals

(A) \(\frac{1}{10}\)

(B) \(\frac{2}{10}\)

(C) \(\frac{3}{10}\)

(D) \(\frac{4}{10}\)

Q.10 Consider the linear regression model \(y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \epsilon_i\); i = 1,2, ..., n, where \(\epsilon_i\)’s are i.i.d. standard normal random variables. Given that

\(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i = 3.2\), \(\quad \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i = 4.2\), \(\quad \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \left( x_j - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \right)^2 = 1.5\) and

\(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{j=1}^{n} \left( x_j - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \right) \left( y_j - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i \right) = 1.7\),

the maximum likelihood estimates of \(\beta_0\) and \(\beta_1\), respectively, are

(A) \(\frac{17}{15}\) and \(\frac{32}{75}\)

(B) \(\frac{32}{75}\) and \(\frac{17}{15}\)

(C) \(\frac{17}{15}\) and \(\frac{43}{75}\)

(D) \(\frac{43}{75}\) and \(\frac{17}{15}\)
