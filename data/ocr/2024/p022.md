Q.27 For \(n \geq 2\), let \(\epsilon_1, \epsilon_2, \ldots, \epsilon_n\) be i.i.d. random variables having the \(N(0,1)\) distribution. Consider \(n\) independent random variables \(Y_1, Y_2, \ldots, Y_n\) defined by
\(Y_i = \beta i + \epsilon_i\), \(i = 1,2, \ldots, n\),
where \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\). Define \(\bar{Y} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} Y_i\), \(T_1 = \frac{2\bar{Y}}{n+1}\), and \(T_2 = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \frac{Y_i}{i}\). Then which of the following statements is NOT correct?

(A) \(T_1\) is an unbiased estimator of \(\beta\)

(B) \(T_2\) is an unbiased estimator of \(\beta\)

(C) \(Var(T_1) < Var(T_2)\)

(D) \(Var(T_1) = Var(T_2)\)
